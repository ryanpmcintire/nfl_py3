## A. The decision line was never the pool's line

**Blast radius: SERVED PICK. Measured.**

The owner's pool is Splash Sports; its spreads lock Tuesday at noon ET and are
posted on the Splash site. The card's decision line came instead from
nflverse's `schedules.spread_line`, which this repo's own docs describe as a
close proxy (`docs/cfb_lead_screens_wave1.md:48`).

The published Week 1 card traced to snapshot `20260905T211016Z`
(`data/raw/.../schedules.parquet`, fetched Sep 5) whose market fields are
stamped 2026-08-18 — three-week-old numbers. Measured against the real Splash
board captured this session (`data/splash/2026_week01_20260908_noon.json`):

| | wrong vs Splash |
|---|---|
| published card (`…124514Z`) | 4 of 16 |
| card regenerated by today's lock (`…162923Z`) | **8 of 16** |

Pulling fresher nflverse data made it worse, because nflverse was never the
right source — it was accidentally close.

**Every Splash line is a half point.** Observed directly on all 16 Week 1
games. No game in this pool can push.

Consequences beyond the line mismatch:

1. `key_line_pick_read` (`KEY_LINE_ATOMS = (3.0, 7.0)`) can only fire on whole
   numbers. It fired on nflverse's 3.0 and 7.0 and **changed 3 of 16 sides**
   on lines the pool does not offer.
2. The served discrete push probability is the probability of an outcome that
   is impossible at a half-point line.
3. The 1,537-game opener archive is graded against Odds-API consensus captured
   at 09:00 ET, not against Splash. The headline accuracy has never been
   measured against the number the pool actually grades.

**Re-run of the promoted stack on the real spreads** (forecast
`2026-week-01-20260908T165428Z`, `synchronization_status: UNLINKED`, all four
sidecars written, active model untouched): **3 picks change** — CHI at CAR
(CHI→CAR), DAL at NYG (DAL→NYG), NO at DET (NO→DET). Exactly the three the
key-line read had flipped.

**Open, untested hypothesis** (owner, 2026-09-08: it "needs to be tested and
verified just like literally everything else in the repo"): a pool line that
differs from the market by half a point may be worth something, because the
half point can straddle a key number. This has NOT been measured and no
accuracy claim is made for it here. It cannot be measured retrospectively —
there is no Splash history — so it can only be assessed prospectively from
Week 1 2026 forward.

## B. The uncertainty machinery manufactures confident negatives

This cluster runs directly against the binding invariant that an interval
containing zero is never grounds for rejection: these defects turn
measurements that said *nothing* into resolved-looking negatives.

> **B1–B4 are FIXED as of 2026-09-08.** The findings below are kept as
> recorded. `docs/weak_signal_pooling.md` documents the corrected machinery,
> the conventions chosen, and the before/after numbers; the fix is covered by
> `tests/test_evidence_conventions.py` and the pooling tests in
> `tests/test_weak_signals.py`. No recorded registry value was rewritten, and
> no line of work was closed by any of it.

### B1. `weak-signals pool` crashes — REPORTED NUMBER, measured

`src/nfl_ats/weak_signals.py:872`. `math.comb(total, k) * 0.5**total`
overflows: the exact int is converted to float and exceeds ~1.8e308. Fine at
n=1020, `OverflowError` at n≥1030; the eligible pool is n=1489.

AGENTS.md instructs every session to *"Re-run the command for the current
number rather than quoting a fixed one here."* That command is currently
unexecutable, so the 2026-09-05 figures quoted there are the only ones anyone
has. Tests pass because every fixture is tiny. The formula itself is correct —
a log-space version reproduces AGENTS.md's `327/628 → p = 0.31846963` exactly.

### B2. Inverse-variance pooling is inverted for this population — REPORTED NUMBER, measured

`weak_signals.py:945-988`. `pooled_effect` weights by `1/SE²` with the SE taken
at face value. Bootstrap band width *shrinks* as a cell gets smaller, so the
least informative entries get the most weight.

`roof_battery_visiting_dome_open_vs_closed_opener` (SE 2.538e-05, **3 games**)
holds **99.997%** of the fixed-effect weight. Its own recorded
`classification_evidence` says verbatim that the narrowness "is an artifact of
resampling a 3-point sample, not statistical power" — the pooler cannot read
that sentence. `--method fixed` therefore reports `excludes_zero: TRUE` on a
value that is that one cell's own estimate to five decimals. `excludes_zero`
is precisely the field a session would cite to justify a terminal verdict.

### B3. The sign test scores exact ties against the candidate — REPORTED NUMBER, measured

`weak_signals.py:286` (`favours_candidate = self.effect > 0.0`), consumed at
`:891`. **208 of 1,489** eligible signals have an effect of exactly zero, and
every one is counted as a win for the baseline.

| | favourable / total | rate | two-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|
| as coded (ties → baseline) | 576 / 1489 | 38.7% | 2.24e-18 |
| ties dropped (standard) | 576 / 1281 | 45.0% | 3.45e-04 |

Both readings lean baseline, so no direction flips — but this is the
difference between "resolved" and "leaning", inflated by fourteen orders of
magnitude on p.

### B4. `probability_positive` charges the zero-atom against the candidate — measured

`clv.py:777`, `overlay_composition.py:371`, and ~40 copy-paste sites:
`np.mean(draws > 0.0)`. The arm and sign are correct; the strict `>` is not. A
paired accuracy delta over a handful of disagreeing picks has a large atom at
exactly zero, and all of it counts as "not positive".

A candidate that makes **identical picks on every game** records
`delta_accuracy 0.0, interval [0,0], probability_positive 0.0` — the strongest
negative the scale can express, for a no-op. **115** such rows sit in the NFL
`accuracy_points` pool; 345 across the registry. Two archived candidates
(`rookie_debut`, `mnf_road`) cross P+ 0.5 on the tie convention alone.

### B5. Validator asymmetry — LOW, measured, 0 live violations

`weak_signals.validate_closure` refuses `wrong_sign_resolved` unless the whole
interval sits below zero, as AGENTS.md requires. `rotation._validate_closing_ground`
checks only that the ground name is admissible and a `probability_positive` is
present — it never inspects the interval. A rotation window could be closed
`closed_negative / wrong_sign_resolved` on an interval straddling zero. Zero
violations today (436 families, 1 correctly-grounded closure).

**Checked and sound** (AUD-2): the resampling unit is the week and blocks are
drawn with replacement; pairing is game-by-game before resampling with common
random numbers; the interval method is percentile at 95% everywhere (zero
basic/reverse-percentile hits repo-wide); the permutation null permutes the
outcome within week with both arms' picks fixed, correctly reproducing the
documented home-tilt offset rather than assuming a zero-centred null;
DerSimonian–Laird is transcribed correctly; `overlay_composition`'s fast path
is proven identical to the slow path to `atol=1e-12`; nothing anywhere
estimates or pads within-week correlation.

## C. Reported numbers that do not reconcile

### C1. Push-excluded accuracy printed against a push-inclusive denominator — REPORTED NUMBER, measured

`handoff.py:214-216`, `public_board.py:3896-3897`, `board_site_content.py:659-663`,
and every doc quoting them.

`opener_accuracy_probability_rule` drops pushes; `metadata["games"]` counts all
scored rows. They are rendered side by side. Measured on
`artifacts/opener_evaluation/20260908T115957Z`: the stated **"54.56% on 1,537
games"** is really **820 / 1,503** (34 pushes excluded). No integer over 1,537
rounds to 54.56%. The correct statement is *"54.56% on 1,503 games (1,537
scored, 34 pushes excluded)."*

`docs/opener_evaluation.md:242-248` gets this right; the rendering code lost it.

### C2. Promoted policy headline numbers are hardcoded literals — REPORTED NUMBER, read

`player_arrests_back_side_overlay.py:45-49` holds `POLICY_OPENER_ACCURACY`,
`POLICY_BASELINE_OPENER_ACCURACY`, `POLICY_EFFECT_ACCURACY_POINTS`,
`POLICY_PROBABILITY_POSITIVE`, `POLICY_GRADED_GAMES` as module constants,
consumed by `handoff.py:221-225` and `public_board.py:1401-1404`. The
arithmetic is internally consistent, but `board_content.verify_number_provenance`
— whose docstring states *"Constants are never an admissible source"* — never
sees them. The eval script that produced them also hardcodes an August 19
opener snapshot as its default input.

### C3. Spliced probability curves are non-monotone — REPORTED NUMBER, measured

`key_line_pick_read.apply_key_line_pick_read_to_sweep` substitutes the lattice
read only on atom rows and leaves the Gaussian elsewhere, so the served line
sweep has **10 of 16 games non-monotone** — home cover probability *rises* as
the home number gets harder (BAL at IND: 0.5718 at −7.5 → 0.6075 at −7.0). The
same splice is in the picks-page slider (`spread_explorer.py:324-353`). The
discrete three-way columns in the same file are clean.

## D. Served-pick defects unrelated to the line source

### D1. Monday-night games vanish from the live opener — SERVED PICK, measured

`market_data.py:514-521` and `clv.py:1291-1294` (the same buggy formula, twice)
derive each game's "own week Tuesday" from the **UTC** weekday of kickoff. A
Monday-night kickoff at 20:15 ET is Tuesday in UTC, so it resolves to the
*following* Tuesday and returns zero opener rows.

Measured: `live_tuesday_openers` returns **15 rows**, missing
`2026_01_DEN_KC`. Downstream, `best_pick_nomination.dispersion_pool_from_frame`
sets `pool_pass = True` for **every** game as soon as one `spread_std` is
missing. Confirmed on this week: **16 games, 1 missing, 16 of 16 pass** — the
Best Pick dispersion filter is silently disabled for the entire week, in any
week with a qualifying Monday-night kickoff.

A correct implementation already exists twice in the repo (`nfl_week.week_cycle_sunday`,
`market_data_halves.current_week_kickoff_window`) and is not reused here.

### D2. The stale-archive guard cannot fire on lock day — SERVED PICK, reported

`home_side_location.py:287-343` matches the opener archive against
`active_ats_model.json` *as it stands when `margin-predict` runs* — the
previous lock's model. The feature table is rebuilt before `margin-predict`, so
the matched evaluation is guaranteed stale and `warnings` comes back empty.
Today: `home_side_offset.json` records `source_model_id: a4c757efd2525da6`
while the card it is attached to is model `9ef62ef158338785`.

### D3. Residual location is in the probability but not the published fair spread — SERVED PICK, reported

`margin.py:1030-1052`. `predicted_margin` / `fair_spread` come from
`_predicted_margin`; `home_cover_probability` is located at
`median(residuals)`. Measured on the locked card the gap is **+0.36905653
points on all 16 rows**. Three of 16 served picks contradict the card's own
published fair spread — e.g. TB at CIN publishes
`predicted_market_residual = −0.086` (CIN fails to cover) while
`home_cover_probability = 0.5089` picks CIN.

### D4. The key-line read's operative mechanism is not the one documented — SERVED PICK, reported

`mass_preserving_lattice.band_read`/`tilted_atoms` match only the first moment
of the band mixture, so the band's line composition and base cover rate
survive. The neutral point is displaced from the line by δ ranging **−1.27 to
+1.44 points** across anchors. Archive confirmation: flips are one-directional
at every atom (25 of 25 to AWAY at |3| and +7) — a push-atom mechanism would
redistribute symmetrically.

The measured cell (+0.200 pts, `probability_positive` 0.7426 on 272 games)
stands; the displacement is inside the measurement. The defect is that
`docs/key_line_pick_read.md` names the push atom while the rule behaves as a
spread-threshold rule, which AGENTS.md's "no unexplained threshold flips"
provision requires be named and bounded.

### D5. The "7" bucket serves a shift against its own admission rationale — SERVED PICK, reported

`home_side_location.py:37-46`. Measured on the fit window today's card used:
the `7` bucket (n=65) has `probability_positive` **0.407** and is served
**−0.171**, while `3.5-6.5` (n=468) at `probability_positive` **0.792** is
excluded. This is not a wrong-sign resolution and closes nothing — the
inconsistency is that the admission rule is not being applied uniformly, and
the `7` bucket's inclusion needs a cited measurement or should be recorded as
an unresolved arm rather than served.

### D6. Silent degradation paths — SERVED PICK, reported

`pick_refresh.py:1582` catches bare `Exception` around the lattice rebuild and
falls back to replaying Tuesday's sidecar, with no log a human would see. A
genuine bug in the lattice reader is indistinguishable from "nothing changed".
Same shape at `home_side_location.py:313`.

## E. Research-measurement defects

### E1. Batteries graded on a mapping production never served — measured

`clv.py:2139` defaulted an absent `probability_method` to the hard-coded
`"ecdf"`, and **35 of 41** call sites build a config dict that omits the key.
The active model serves `gaussian_median`. Every battery cell routed through
those scripts was therefore graded on ECDF — neither the served mapping nor the
discrete lattice. A validated patch resolves the key from the active manifest
and fails closed when it cannot (`resolve_active_probability_method`), records
the resolved mapping on the returned frame, and adds a source-scan guard test.

### E2. Same-week self-inclusion in the opener walk-forward — measured, inert

`clv.py:2141-2145` takes the cutoff from the *paired* subset's earliest
kickoff, not the week's. Production (`outcomes.py:504-511`) takes the whole
week. Exactly one occurrence in 1,537 archived games (2021 week 2, an unpaired
Thursday game): max |Δ home_cover_probability| **0.00246**, **0 pick flips**.
Real defect, zero measured consequence.

### E3. Challenger arms confounding two policies — reported

`ecdf_mapping_incumbent_overlay` and `gaussian_mean_mapping_incumbent_overlay`
replace `home_cover_probability` on every game including the key-line games,
so the recorded delta is (mapping change) + (dropping the key-line read).
`era_weighted_half_life_8_overlay` has a third behaviour again. Up to 3 of 16
picks this week differ for a reason that is not the policy under test.
