Track record

How good is this, honestly?

One record, one story, told start to finish.

THE PROJECT

What this is

Every week this project picks a side for every NFL game against the point spread, before kickoff. It is one person's research project: a model makes a pick, a few hand-built rules overrule it when they fire, and every claim on this page is graded against what actually happened.

THE MODEL

The model

The model does not predict football from scratch. It starts from the market's own spread — the sharpest number in sports — and predicts by how much the actual game will differ from that line. Its inputs are team strength, weighted toward recent weeks, and who is actually available to play: practices missed, players ruled out. The research log calls this “weak_stack / market_residual”. To a human: a beats-the-line model that respects injuries.

MEASURED

What it has done

Asked to pick winners against Tuesday's opening lines for the last six seasons, the bare model was right 53.4% of the time. A coin flip is 50%. On the same games, adding the fix-up rules — fade newly-coached teams, adjust for arrested players, fade revenge-game and trap-line spots — lifted it to 54.2%. That is the measured history.

PLANNING ESTIMATE

What to expect going forward

There is a catch, and it matters: those fix-up rules were chosen by looking at the same history they are graded on. Any rule picked as best on a dataset flatters that dataset. Discounted for that, the honest expectation for the full card is ≈55% against Tuesday lines. Not a promise — a planning number, and the 2026 season is graded against it in real time.
The selection discount, in numbers

Coin flip: 50%. Raw model before policy overlays: 53.4% at the opener (1,537 games, 2020-2025); 52.1% at the sharper close.

Played chain (raw → coach fade → arrests): 54.2% measured on 1,503 paired games — the measured history under the crowned expectation.

Fix-up rules: paired +1.26 points on reused data (P+ 0.857); its 55.4% archive score is selection-inflated (best of 127 subsets), and the out-of-sample re-check of that selection measured 0.00 pts (P+ 0.49) — already discounted in the ≈55% expectation.

Movement rule (market-follow on >=1pt moves via refresh): composed +1.53 points (P+ 0.89/0.93) — an attribution upper bound on already-looked-at data.

Best documented long-run bettors: roughly 55-56% against the close. Measured pregame ceiling: about 56% (total-leak control, docs/leak_ceiling_control.md); the older 57-58% band was the pre-measurement guess.

A small step above a coin flip is not proof of a stable, profitable edge — sportsbook vig alone would likely erase it. These are forced paper picks — not a game-level probability, not a profit claim.

TWO LINES, ONE RECORD

The two lines

Every pick is graded against two lines. The Tuesday opener is what we actually pick against — being early is the whole skill. The closing line is the market's final word after a week of injury news and money, and it is the hardest test in sports: 52.1% against the close is the same body of work, measured the harsh way. Both are published because a record that only beats soft lines is not an edge.

FALSIFIABILITY

How we would know we are wrong

Starting with the Week 1 lock on September 8, 2026, every pick is written to a tamper-evident ledger before kickoff and settled after, at both lines. If the season lands at or below 50%, the honest conclusion is that the edge is not real. No re-tuning, no excuses — the ledger is the referee.

How the picks are graded

The model baseline and played policy

The raw model probability rule -- the baseline beneath today's card: pick whichever team the model gives at least a 50% chance to cover. It scores 53.4% at the opener on this archive -- this is the opener baseline quoted in the story's MEASURED section above.

The played policy: apply the year-1-coach policy, then the promoted player-arrest policy. The arrest component's frozen opener evaluation scored 53.76% versus 53.36% on 1,503 graded games (+0.399 accuracy points, P+ 0.86). It remains unresolved and paired prospective tracking continues.

The sign rule -- the original grading protocol: pick whichever team the model's single point forecast favors, a slightly different question (the prediction's midpoint rather than its full probability) that was never used to choose a real pick. Graded the same way on the same games, it scores 52.8% on the same games. Both are reported on purpose, every time -- see docs/opener_evaluation.md for why they can differ.

How to read this honestly

Four things that keep these numbers honest

Nothing here was picked after seeing the answer. Every number is measured on games the model never trained on, with the recipe frozen before the games were scored.

A single number is the middle of a range. The pool grade's honest range runs from about 52.0% to 54.6% across seasons. The headline is the best single guess; the range is where the true skill could plausibly sit.

One look, once. Each headline came from a single planned measurement of a frozen model. Re-running a test until it finally looks good is the fastest way to fool yourself, so we do not do it.

52-53% against a frozen line is genuinely good. Realistically excellent is 54-55%. Someone who knew everything knowable before kickoff would top out near 57-58% against a frozen Tuesday line, because football itself is noisy -- final margins scatter about 13.5 points around even a perfect expectation. If this page ever shows 60%, that is a bug to hunt, not a breakthrough.

The ceiling arithmetic behind that last paragraph is written up in docs/pool_edge_plan.md.
